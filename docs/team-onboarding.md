# noClue Research — team onboarding

## The one workflow

```text
Ask → plan → approve sources → collect → save proof → choose a person → request contact → send manually → record result
```

Use the sidebar from top to bottom. Do not skip proof.

## Pages at a glance

| Page | Use it for | What you do there |
|---|---|---|
| **Overview** | Understand the workflow | Start a new piece of research. |
| **Research** | Plan and collect | Describe what you need, edit the plan, approve sources, run collection. |
| **Collections** | Check what happened | Open **View trace** to see every source, result, and review note. |
| **Records** | Keep useful people and companies | Save only candidates worth revisiting. Attach their proof. |
| **Knowledge** | Reuse event context and evidence | Read stored evidence. The document vault is the next feature. |
| **Outreach** | Find a work contact after review | Pick one saved person and choose one contact provider. |
| **Playbook** | Learn as a team | Record message results, objections, and what worked. |

## Research: exact steps

| Step | Do this | Good example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Describe the outcome | Write one clear request. | “Find 15 Bangalore AI product leaders who can explain a real production failure.” |
| 2. Review the plan | Correct the type, city, target count, and evaluation criteria. | Set type to **Speaker** and city to **Bangalore**. |
| 3. Save the question | Save only when the brief says exactly what you need. | “AI engineering leaders for Shipped Live.” |
| 4. Approve sources | Add allowed domains and exact public pages. | A company’s official blog, event agenda, or speaker profile. |
| 5. Run collection | Click **Collect pages**. | The tool reads only the pages you approved. |
| 6. Inspect trace | Open **Collections → View trace**. | Check collected pages and anything marked “needs review.” |
| 7. Save candidates | In **Records**, add the useful person or organisation. | Add why they fit, their city, role, company, and public profile. |

## What counts as good proof?

Save proof that supports the exact decision.

| Goal | Useful proof |
|---|---|
| Speaker | A shipped product, technical talk, public write-up, open-source work, or credible event appearance. |
| Sponsor | Relevant customer, developer, hiring, employer-brand, or regional business objective. |
| Venue | City office/event-space evidence, community-event history, or suitable team contact route. |
| Attendee | Relevant role, active build, community contribution, or clear interest in the city theme. |

## Outreach: use only after a human review

1. Open **Records** and confirm why this person matters.
2. Add a public LinkedIn address or company domain.
3. Open **Outreach** and choose the person.
4. Choose **one** provider:

| Provider | Use it when |
|---|---|
| Apollo | You need a work email, or a work phone lookup if enabled. |
| RocketReach | You need email and phone, and accept its credit use. |
| Hunter | You know the company domain and need a work-email route. |
| Surfe | You have a suitable public profile and need professional contact enrichment. |

5. Check the returned result before drafting or sending.
6. Send manually. The platform must never send a message on its own.

## Three non-negotiable rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Do not treat a model suggestion as a fact. | Only sources prove claims. |
| Do not add pages you do not have permission to research. | Collection is limited to approved public pages. |
| Do not use a paid contact lookup for an unqualified person. | Contact-provider credits are valuable and personal outreach should be deliberate. |

## When you finish a task

Update the record with one of these outcomes:

```text
Not suitable → save reason
Suitable, no contact route → keep researching
Contact found → prepare personalised draft
Message sent → log date and angle
Replied / declined / meeting booked → add outcome and objection
```

That final update is what makes the next team member faster.
